# POLYWORDS Content Doctrine

## Goal

Create recognition-memory writing that produces:

> I should have known that.

The player should experience a subconscious smirk, not an obscure-fact ambush.

Write with the precision of a well-made crossword clue, but for POLYWORDS recognition memory. Approach the memory indirectly, make every word earn its place, and create brief productive hesitation before the connection becomes exact and inevitable. Do not import crossword obscurity, abbreviations, specialist trivia, or complicated wordplay.

## Meaning Audit

Before writing:
1. Audit authoritative dictionary definitions.
2. Identify distinct meanings.
3. Merge duplicate or overlapping senses.
4. Separate true meanings from common phrases, collocations, extensions, and associations.
5. Approve the meaning inventory before drafting REALs.

Classify legitimate senses for playability before writing. Create exactly one REAL per locked playable sense. A real but obscure, overly regional, weakly recognizable, or unwritable sense may be held or excluded rather than forced into weak gameplay. Do not inflate the inventory by treating every familiar phrase as a separate sense.

## Interactive Writing Sequence

When building a new word with Pete:
1. Present the audited definitions and sense decisions first.
2. Present the human handles or memory surfaces for those senses.
3. Draft REAL masks only from the approved senses and strongest approved angles.
4. Work interactively, preserving every line Pete locks and redirecting only the line under discussion.
5. After the REAL set is stable, build an independent word-level trap pool.
6. Run headword, ownership, angle-diversity, cross-contamination, and canonical-workbook checks before proposing or locking any tile.
7. Update a workbook only when Pete explicitly asks, using the newest supplied workbook and only the wording he approved.

If the current canonical workbook is unavailable, label the writing `PROVISIONAL`. Do not claim that the database comparison passed, and do not lock the tiles until the check can be completed.

## REAL Standard

A REAL must:
- represent a legitimate distinct meaning
- avoid dictionary-definition phrasing
- use a recognizable human memory snap: a scene, action, sound, saying, object, ritual, or moment where the meaning lives
- disguise without removing the recognition trigger
- sound natural
- create an immediate snap on reveal
- remain fair for the assigned difficulty tier
- avoid the headword and revealing derivatives
- use a meaningfully different recognition angle from every other tile on the same word

## Length Rule

There is no maximum word count for REAL masks or TRAP cards. Eight words is not a cap.

Use as many words as the memory snap genuinely needs to:
- sound natural
- remain fair
- identify the intended memory precisely
- produce strong recognition

Shorter is better only when it is equally effective. Never weaken, flatten, distort, or make a tile unnatural merely to satisfy a number.

Recognition angles may include:
- everyday situations
- familiar objects
- common sayings
- social behavior
- work settings
- sports
- physical imagery
- durable cultural references
- school knowledge
- widely encountered technology

## Trap Standard

A trap must be written specifically for the exact headword.

It may arise from:
- a neighboring concept
- a common association
- a familiar phrase that is not a true meaning
- sound or spelling
- imagery
- cultural memory
- false category membership
- reversal or opposition
- an almost-meaning

Build one independent trap pool for the headword as a whole. Do not pair traps one-to-one with REALs, dress traps in a REAL's scene, or manufacture a set of mirrors, opposites, or negations. A trap may happen to involve opposition only when it independently tempts the headword and passes every ownership test.

A valid trap:
- is tempting for a clear human reason
- feels plausible for this headword specifically
- is not another legitimate meaning
- has one clear true-owner word
- makes that true owner feel immediately correct when substituted or revealed
- becomes clearly wrong after examination or reveal
- produces “I can see why I picked that,” not “That was nonsense”
- avoids the headword and revealing derivatives

Reject generic traps that could be pasted under many unrelated words.

## Headword Rule

Never use the headword inside a REAL or trap.

Also reject obvious:
- inflections
- tense variants
- plural forms
- adverb forms
- compounds
- derivatives

when they reveal the answer.

Judge semantically, not only by exact string matching.

The Headword Rule is constitutional and cannot be waived through the general Exception Rule.

## Wording, Angle, and Memory-Snap Separation

Every tile on one headword must approach from a meaningfully different angle. Do not recycle the same subject, situation, object, sentence frame, key image, or recognition moment merely by changing a few words.

Enforce intra-word cross-contamination separation between REALS and traps:
- Do not reuse distinctive content words or phrases.
- Do not reuse the same subject, scene, object, or memory snap.
- Do not let a trap borrow a REAL's recognition trigger.
- Do not let a REAL telegraph the neighborhood used by a trap.
- Ordinary connective words do not count as contamination.

This intra-word separation is a constitutional card-building rule, not a stylistic preference. Do not use the general Exception Rule to waive it.

Also cross-reference proposed distinctive wording, subjects, scenes, and memory snaps against the full current canonical workbook. Across different headwords, avoid recognizable recycling whenever a natural alternative exists. Similar real-life territory is acceptable only when the tile is written differently enough that it does not feel reused.

## Angle-First Rule

Write each tile from the strongest available human-recognition angle for that exact word.

Do not force all words into the same template.

Within a card, do not force several meanings through the same angle. Each tile should wake a different memory rather than repeat one successful formula.

The angle may be visual, situational, social, physical, cultural, emotional, occupational, or phrase-based. Choose the angle that produces the strongest fair recognition.

## Exception Rule

Rules protect quality; they do not replace editorial judgment.

An exception may be approved only when it is clearly more:
- human
- fair
- specific to the word
- memorable
- playable
- recognition-rich

than the compliant alternatives.

An exception must be deliberate and special, not merely convenient.

Record why the exception improves the set.

## Fairness Tiers

### Core
Most American English speakers should recognize the sense after reveal.

### Advanced
Recognizable to many players, but less universal.

### Expert or Bonus
Specialized, regional, archaic, technical, historical, or culturally narrow material that players knowingly opt into.

Dictionary validity alone does not make content appropriate for core play.

## Rejection Conditions

Reject a REAL when:
- the sense is technically valid but culturally invisible
- recognition requires specialist training in core content
- the wording sounds like a dictionary
- the recognition trigger has been removed
- the clue could point to many unrelated concepts
- the sense requires a long defense after reveal
- it is a phrase but not a distinct meaning

Reject a trap when:
- it is another real meaning
- it is obviously unrelated
- it uses random association
- it depends on a cheap grammatical trick
- it is harder to understand than the REALs
- it is generic rather than word-specific
- the player cannot see why it was tempting

## Editorial Review Questions

For every tile ask:
1. Is this a legitimate REAL or a legitimate trap?
2. Is it specifically right for this headword?
3. Does it sound human?
4. Is the recognition angle strong enough?
5. Is it fair at the assigned tier?
6. Does the headword or a revealing derivative appear?
7. Does this tile use a meaningfully different angle from the other tiles on the card?
8. Does it cross-contaminate distinctive wording, a subject, scene, object, or memory snap between a REAL and a trap?
9. Has its distinctive wording or memory snap been cross-referenced against the current canonical workbook?
10. Is it as tight as a good crossword clue without sacrificing naturalness or fairness?
11. Is the length justified by the recognition moment rather than an arbitrary number?
12. For a trap, is there one clear true owner and does substitution feel immediately correct?
13. Does the reveal produce “I should have known that”?
14. Would Pete recognize the wording as natural and playable?
15. Is an exception being used, and has it earned its place?

## Daily Challenge Content Standard

The Hunt doctrine above does not govern Daily Challenge content. Use the following standalone
fallback when the repository's canonical `docs/DAILY_CONTENT_WRITING_STANDARD.md` is unavailable.

### Source Discipline

- Canonical authoring workbook:
  `workbooks/POLYWORDS_Daily_Challenge_Locked_2026-08-24.xlsx`
- Runtime representation: `app/game/dailyPool.ts`
- The workbook is the authoring truth. Runtime is an exported representation.
- When memory, chat history, runtime, and the current locked workbook disagree about approved
  source wording or status, inspect the canonical workbook rather than guessing.

### Meaning Gate

1. Write for American English.
2. Audit authoritative dictionary definitions before drafting.
3. Merge duplicate or overlapping senses.
4. Separate true meanings from phrases, collocations, examples, associations, and grammatical
   restatements.
5. Require exactly three genuinely distinct, broadly recognizable meanings for every Daily
   target.
6. If only two strong meanings exist, reject the target rather than inventing or stretching a
   third.

`SENTENCE` is the controlling precedent for this rejection rule.

### Interactive Writing Order

For a new Daily target:

1. Complete the definitions and meaning audit.
2. Select the three meanings.
3. Identify the human handles and recognition surfaces.
4. Draft the three clues.
5. Work interactively and preserve every line Pete locks.
6. Order the clues hardest to easiest.
7. Once the clues are stable, build the nine-word source grid.
8. Audit the complete clue/grid set.
9. Lock only after explicit approval.
10. Update the canonical workbook only from approved wording.

### Clue Standard

- Daily clues are more direct than Hunt REAL writing, but still target recognition memory rather
  than dictionary prose.
- Use familiar situations, objects, actions, roles, sayings, and everyday knowledge.
- Use a different recognition surface for each clue.
- Order the clues hardest, middle, easiest.
- There is no hard word-count cap. Prefer brevity only when it is equally natural, fair, and
  precise.
- Do not make Daily clues artificially obscure merely to imitate Hunt disguise.

### Headword Rule

- The target may not appear inside a clue.
- Reject obvious revealing inflections, derivatives, compounds, and morphological variants.
- Any deliberate exception requires Pete's explicit approval. Record it narrowly; never weaken
  the general rule to accommodate one exception.

### Nine-Word Source Grid

Each approved source entry contains exactly nine unique candidate words: the target exactly once
and eight distractors. The runtime may deterministically present the target plus five distractors
in an actual round, but editorial approval applies to all nine source candidates.

Build distractors for the target and puzzle as a whole. Distractors:

- are not rigidly paired one-to-one with clues
- spread temptation across the three clue domains
- are plausible from at least one clue or semantic neighborhood
- fail the full three-clue convergence
- do not create a second defensible complete answer
- are recognizable American-English words
- do not need to be polysemous or come from the POLYWORDS master headword database
- do not rely on cheap morphology or spelling gimmicks

### Cross-Contamination

Audit the entire three-clue, nine-word set. Do not reuse distinctive clue wording, phrases,
subjects, scenes, objects, or memory snaps when reuse telegraphs the answer, copies a recognition
trigger, or makes a distractor mechanically obvious.

- Do not let one clue reuse another clue's recognition surface.
- A distractor may attack the same semantic neighborhood as a clue through different language.
- Ordinary connective or common language is not contamination.
- Cross-reference distinctive wording, scenes, subjects, and memory snaps against the current
  canonical Daily workbook and the wider locked POLYWORDS corpus when available.
- Avoid recognizable recycling whenever a natural alternative exists, but do not mechanically
  ban ordinary semantic overlap.

### Fairness Audit Before Lock

Verify:

1. Exactly three distinct meanings.
2. All three are recognizable enough for Daily.
3. Clues are ordered hardest to easiest.
4. Each clue sounds human.
5. Each clue represents a different meaning.
6. There is no target/headword leakage.
7. There are exactly nine unique candidates.
8. The target appears exactly once.
9. Every distractor has an honest temptation reason.
10. Every distractor fails the full clue set.
11. There is no second defensible answer.
12. There is no material clue/grid cross-contamination.
13. The canonical workbook cross-reference is complete.
14. Any exception is explicit, narrow, recorded, and improves gameplay.

### Locking

- Only explicit Pete approval locks content.
- “Lock it,” “lock it next,” and explicit batch approval preserve exactly the approved wording.
- Never silently rewrite locked clues or grid words.
- Intentional easier clues or gimmes are allowed when deliberately used for pacing or confidence.
