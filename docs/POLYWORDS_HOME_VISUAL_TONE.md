# POLYWORDS Home Visual Tone

**Status:** Locked product direction unless Pete explicitly reopens it  
**Applies to:** Home screen / Polly's room, Home background art, Home props, Home transitions, Polybook presentation, Hunt departure, Daily Challenge departure  
**Purpose:** Preserve the intended personality, atmosphere, and visual language of the POLYWORDS Home so this direction does not need to be re-explained in future art, design, or implementation work.

---

## 1. Core Concept

The POLYWORDS Home screen is **Polly's room**.

It is not a generic castle lobby, throne room, fantasy hub, royal hall, cathedral chamber, or polished menu environment.

Polly broke out of the cage that was holding her there. She still lives in this abandoned castle and has effectively claimed the place as her own. The room should feel like a forgotten castle chamber that Polly has adopted, occupied, and mentally promoted herself into ruling.

The core attitude is:

> **The castle is genuinely worn. Polly's attitude toward it is not.**

She is not sad to be there. She is having the time of her life. She flies around, laughs at the player, cracks jokes, wears a crown, and carries herself like she is the king of the place even though the kingdom is basically falling apart around her.

That contrast is central to the Home screen's identity.

---

## 2. Emotional Tone

The Home should feel:

- mischievous
- lightly haunted
- weathered
- unruly
- lived-in
- playful without being childish
- campy without being corny
- magical without becoming grandiose
- abandoned without becoming depressing
- stylized without becoming cartoonish
- funny through attitude and environmental irony, not gag props

The game itself has jokes, campy music, Polly's sarcasm, and a playful tone. The Home art must support that personality rather than contradict it with cinematic gloom, tragic ruins, or overly serious fantasy drama.

A useful shorthand is:

> **A once-grand castle room that has gone a little feral, and Polly is having the time of her life in it.**

Another acceptable shorthand:

> **Worn magical castle, lightly haunted, slightly unruly, warm enough to feel like home, strange enough to feel like Polly's.**

---

## 3. What the Room Is Not

Do **not** drift into any of these visual lanes:

- grimdark fantasy
- gothic cathedral
- polished royal throne room
- luxury fantasy castle
- shiny clean game lobby
- generic medieval chamber
- ornate high-fantasy architecture
- children's-cartoon castle
- horror ruin
- sad post-apocalyptic abandonment
- dramatic cinematic fantasy key art

The Home should never feel more dramatic than Polly herself.

The environment sets up Polly's personality. It should not compete with her.

---

## 4. Abandonment Without Depression

The castle is abandoned and neglected, but the art should communicate **decay without despair**.

Appropriate signs of neglect include:

- chipped and cracked stone
- cracked crown emblems
- damaged or split books
- bent or worn metal
- broken trim
- crooked fixtures
- uneven stonework
- vines or roots growing through masonry
- haunted or corrupted bricks
- dust and minor debris in corners
- aged repairs
- worn surfaces
- subtle magical residue

These elements should be controlled. Do not cover every surface with cracks, vines, glowing magic, dust, broken objects, and debris at once. That produces visual noise and turns the room into a ruin showcase.

The room needs **selective evidence of abandonment**, not destruction porn.

---

## 5. Polly's Relationship to the Room

Polly has claimed the room.

The room should quietly suggest that she considers herself the ruler of this forgotten place.

That attitude should come through indirectly:

- the broken cage feels like a trophy or historical scar, not a tragic object
- her perch should feel almost like her throne without literally becoming a throne
- the Polybook feels like her personal book, journal, ledger, or prized possession
- cracked crowns and royal imagery become slightly funny because Polly still proudly wears a crown
- the room may feel a little crooked or improvised, but Polly acts as though everything is exactly as it should be

Humor should come from this **environmental irony**.

Do not fill the room with obvious joke props or visual punchlines. Polly is already the loud personality. The environment should be the straight man.

---

## 6. The Visual Humor Rule

The Home art should be amusing without looking like it is trying to be funny.

Strong examples of the right kind of humor:

- a cracked crown motif that Polly still treats as royal
- a slightly crooked perch she treats like a throne
- a shelf leaning a little farther than seems wise
- a vine wrapping around something that was once formal or important
- a book stack that looks barely stable
- the broken cage remaining as evidence that containing Polly did not go according to plan

The humor must come from the world being **slightly off**, not from exaggerated cartoon staging.

Avoid:

- oversized comedy props
- goofy faces in architecture
- joke signs
- slapstick clutter
- childish exaggeration
- whimsical fantasy clichés that make the room look like a children's app

---

## 7. Art Style Rules

The Home must be built from the **existing POLYWORDS visual language**, especially the current `play-screen-overhaul` art.

Do not use generic fantasy-castle design as a substitute for POLYWORDS art direction.

### Architecture

Use the game's established architecture as the authority:

- chunky, readable stone forms
- broad shapes
- simple construction
- restrained ornamentation
- familiar POLYWORDS arches and stone language
- minimal decorative complexity

Do not invent ornate gothic windows, cathedral columns, elaborate gold filigree, fantasy-palace trim, or unrelated medieval architecture simply because it looks appropriate for a castle.

### Materials

Materials should feel believable but stylized:

- painted stone
- worn wood
- aged metal
- parchment and books
- restrained magical surfaces

Avoid plastic gloss, excessive shine, heavy cinematic rendering, or hyper-real material treatment.

### Color

Use the actual POLYWORDS palette and reference art as authority, not approximate fantasy purples.

The room should live primarily in the game's violet / indigo / dark-purple stone family, with restrained warm gold accents.

Gold should be used selectively for meaningful details, not as an overall warm lighting wash.

### Lighting

Lighting should be:

- soft
- readable
- moody without being dramatic
- cool enough to connect to the existing castle world
- warm only in small controlled accents

Avoid:

- theatrical spotlighting
- heavy orange candlelight
- cinematic rim-light drama
- oppressive darkness
- glossy golden ambience

The room is abandoned, not haunted-house horror.

---

## 8. Home Spatial Logic

The Home screen is a real physical place with clear spatial logic.

### Home / Polly's Room
This is the persistent base location.

### Polybook
The player is still in Polly's room.

The Polybook is a physical object that belongs there. When selected, it comes forward and becomes the experience, but conceptually the player has not left Home.

### Hunt
The player leaves Polly's room by going **down / inward** through the Hunt route.

The Home environment should support the idea that Hunt lies beneath or deeper inside the castle.

### Daily Challenge
The player leaves Polly's room by going **out through the window** toward the Daily Castle.

The Daily Castle visible outside the window must be the actual established Daily Castle, or a faithful distant representation of that exact structure. Do not invent a different castle.

This spatial model is part of the Home identity:

> **Hunt goes down. Daily goes out. Polybook comes forward.**

---

## 9. Home State Behavior

The collapsed / quiet desk state is part of the **app-open entrance sequence**, not the permanent Home navigation state.

### First app open in a session

1. Home appears in its calm initial state.
2. Polly flies in.
3. The cage is hanging from / near the POLYWORDS logo.
4. The cage rattles.
5. The cage falls.
6. The cage breaks.
7. After a short beat, the three navigation compartments stagger out.

### Normal Home state during the session

Once the navigation compartments are out, they stay functionally available for the session.

When the player selects a destination, the relevant compartments may retract as part of the transition. When the player returns Home, the navigation compartments come back out automatically.

Do not require the player to tap the desk or discover a hidden interaction to reveal navigation.

The clean collapsed state is cinematic staging, not a navigation gate.

---

## 10. Background-First Production Rule

Build the Home environment as a **master background plate first**.

Do not attempt to solve every Home state as a single finished illustration.

The base background should establish:

- room proportions
- wall and floor treatment
- window placement
- architectural language
- closed desk / compartment structure
- closed Hunt floor / mechanism area
- Polly's perch zone
- cage hanging zone
- room wear and abandonment
- visual relationship to the Daily Castle outside

Moving or state-specific elements should remain separate whenever possible:

- Polly
- cage
- broken cage pieces
- navigation compartments / labels
- Polybook
- Hunt opening / hallway reveal
- transition effects

This keeps the environment consistent across Home states and makes animation practical.

---

## 11. Composition and Personality Balance

The Home should feel personal without turning into a Polly-themed souvenir shop.

Use only a few strong personal anchors, such as:

- broken cage history
- Polybook resting place
- Polly perch / throne-like zone
- one or two restrained crown, feather, or PW details

Do not stamp the crown, feather, logo, or PW motif across every surface.

The strongest personality should come from **how Polly has occupied the abandoned room**, not from branding every object.

---

## 12. Visual Quality Test

A successful Home background should pass all of these checks:

### World fit
If shown beside the Daily Castle, Hunt environment, Polly art, Polybook, and other current POLYWORDS screens, it should clearly look like the same game and the same world.

### Polly fit
If Polly is placed into the room, she should look native to it rather than pasted onto a separate art style.

### Tone fit
The room should feel abandoned and lightly haunted without feeling tragic, scary, or oppressive.

### Humor fit
The room should have a faint visual smirk without obvious joke props or childish comedy.

### Authority fit
The environment may imply old royalty, but Polly's current presence should make it feel like she has unofficially taken over.

### Simplicity fit
The environment must remain readable behind navigation and animation. It should not become an ornate fantasy illustration.

### Identity test
A useful question:

> **If Polly were removed from the screen, would the room still feel like a mildly ridiculous abandoned kingdom somebody has confidently taken over?**

If yes, the tone is close.

---

## 13. Final North Star

The Home should look like **Polly's claimed room inside an abandoned, lightly haunted POLYWORDS castle**.

It is worn, cracked, crooked, and neglected, but not miserable.

Polly treats it like her kingdom.

The visuals should carry the same restrained camp, personality, and confidence as the music, jokes, and character writing without becoming childish, corny, overly cartoonish, or dramatically gothic.

The result should feel like:

> **A forgotten castle that should probably be sad, except Polly lives there now and has decided she is king.**

That contradiction is the Home screen's personality.
