# POLYWORDS Content Writing Standard

## Authority

This document is the source of truth for meaning inventories, REAL masks, traps, hidden
meanings, and editorial approval.

It consolidates the approved guidance from:

1. `WORDwriting.docx` as the primary writing authority;
2. `GOLDEN RULE.docx` as the legality and fairness checklist;
3. `maskewriterGPT.docx` only where it does not conflict with the first two.

## The Game Test

The player must feel:

> I should have known that.

Never:

> How was I supposed to know that?

A REAL is not a shortened definition and not a riddle. It is one legally true meaning shown
through a visual, human mask. A trap uses the same voice but lands on the nearby wrong thing.

Apply the gates in this order:

1. **Legality:** the REAL is one actual meaning, or the trap is clearly not a meaning.
2. **Fairness:** the connection feels inevitable after reveal.
3. **Masking:** the wording makes the brain reach without hiding the evidence.
4. **Human voice:** the phrase sounds like something a person could naturally picture or say.
5. **Hidden Truth parity:** wording never reveals REAL versus trap before commitment.
6. **Format:** uppercase, stable IDs, and eight words maximum.

Bad clever is poison. Clean clever is gold.

POLYWORDS uses contemporary American English:

- Use American spellings, vocabulary, idioms, and ordinary American usage.
- Do not approve a British-only spelling, phrase, pronunciation, or meaning.
- A meaning found in a broad international dictionary is only a candidate until an
  American-English source confirms that American players can fairly recognize it.
- Regional American usage may be used only when it is familiar enough to remain fair.

## Core Standard

POLYWORDS is not a vocabulary quiz. The player already knows the hero word and recognizes its
meanings through masked human context.

The target reaction is:

> Wait...
>
> Oh. Right.

A tile should feel inevitable after reveal, not obvious before it.

## Locked Tile Rules

- Tiles may use up to eight words. Eight words is the hard maximum.
- There is no preferred short length. Do not force tiles into three or four words.
- Use the full eight-word space when it makes a tile more playable, more human, more tricky,
  or less definition-like.
- A tile must mask the meaning, not define it.
- The goal is hesitation, not confusion.
- Creativity is required, but it must sound like natural human language.
- Creative does not mean abstract, poetic, cute, or weird.
- Do not use fake object-acting unless the phrase is already natural, idiomatic, cultural, or
  genuinely funny.
- No mask or trap may leak the headword, an obvious inflection, or a giveaway root.
- Traps must be guilty-close, tempting, and legally wrong.
- A trap cannot be a real meaning of the word.
- REAL masks and traps should have similar length, tone, and specificity so wording does not
  reveal the answer.

## Mandatory Tile Audit Gate

Before any REAL/MASK/TRAP batch is delivered, patched, or approved, the writer must self-audit
every tile against the full POLYWORDS standard.

The audit must check all rules together. Do not improve one rule while forgetting another.

A tile fails if any required answer is "no":

1. Is it 8 words or fewer?
2. Does it avoid the headword, inflections, and giveaway roots?
3. Does it sound like natural human language?
4. Does it mask the meaning instead of defining it?
5. Does it create hesitation, not confusion?
6. Is it creative without becoming abstract, poetic, cute, or weird?
7. Does it avoid fake object-acting?
8. Is every REAL tied to a real meaning of the word?
9. Is every TRAP guilty-close but legally wrong?
10. For every TRAP, is the answer "no" to this challenge: Could a player fairly argue that the
    TRAP is actually a real meaning?
11. Do REALS and TRAPS have similar tone, length, and specificity?
12. Are key nouns, roots, settings, or semantic scenery repeated too much?

If any tile fails the audit, rewrite it before presenting or patching it.

For writing passes, prefer small controlled batches of 5 words until quality is stable. Do not
perform large data rewrites without sample approval.

Unacceptable tiles that should have failed this audit:

- `PAPER FOLDED OVER AT THE CREASE`
- `LOSING HAND, BIGGER BET`
- `PHONE BEGS FOR OUTLET`
- `TOTAL JUMPS AT THE BOTTOM`
- `CHARGER LOST BEHIND THE COUCH`

The first two are too flat or mechanical and do not create enough game hesitation. `PHONE BEGS
FOR OUTLET` and `TOTAL JUMPS AT THE BOTTOM` use fake object-acting and unnatural human phrasing.
`CHARGER LOST BEHIND THE COUCH` leaks the hero word's root.

## Meaning Inventory Gate

Writing begins only after the meaning inventory is approved.

Every meaning must:

- be a genuinely distinct dictionary sense;
- have a short editorial definition;
- include a source name and source URL;
- be fair, recognizable, playable, and meaningfully different;
- avoid dusty specialist trivia and predictable grammatical variations;
- be approved by a human before generation.

Ask whether a dictionary would likely give the sense its own numbered entry. Merge examples,
subtypes, grammatical variations, and predictable extensions.

Cover every strong playable meaning, but do not inflate a word with dictionary dust. The
approved ordinary meaning count determines `wordType`; legacy `wordType` values are not trusted.

## REAL Mask Standard

A REAL mask represents exactly one true meaning.

A REAL must be:

- legally true;
- fair and recognizable after reveal;
- natural and no longer than eight words;
- a familiar human, cultural, legal, medical, sporting, historical, or everyday handle;
- indirect enough to create recognition without becoming a riddle.

A REAL must not be:

- a definition, synonym, explanation, or textbook label;
- a nearby object, result, association, or vibe pretending to be the meaning;
- a fake mini-scene or cute object acting;
- a duplicate of another meaning;
- a combination of multiple meanings;
- a phrase containing the headword or an obvious inflection.

Good angles include cultural handles, common phrases, pressure moments, familiar object
placements, and real-world labels. Use the angle people naturally carry in memory.

Examples:

- ADDRESS: `GPS DESTINATION`
- ADDRESS: `PRESIDENTIAL SPEECH`
- ARMS: `RIGHT TO BEAR THEM`
- BALANCE: `SCALES OF JUSTICE`
- BALL: `CINDERELLA'S MIDNIGHT DANCE`
- BELT: `ONE MORE NOTCH AFTER DINNER`

## Disguise, Do Not Define

The mask should make the brain reach. It must still resolve cleanly to the meaning after reveal.

Too flat:

- WAVE: `A SURFER RIDES IT`
- CAST: `THROW A FISHING LINE`
- BANK: `SIDE OF A RIVER`

Clean masked direction:

- WAVE: `SURFER'S PERFECT RIDE`
- CAST: `FISHING LINE LEAVES HAND`
- BANK: `RIVER'S MUDDY SHOULDER`

Overwritten:

- BLADE: `WHERE THE KNIFE DOES ITS WORST`
- BOOT: `BLACK SCREEN FINALLY WAKES UP`
- BED: `ALARM CLOCK'S FAVORITE HOSTAGE`

These are forced mini-riddles. They sound written instead of human.

Strong masks usually use a familiar phrase, recognizable pressure moment, visual action, cultural
handle, or clean metaphor. The image must belong to the meaning itself. A nearby object, result,
or setting belongs in the trap bank instead.

Use as much of the eight-word space as the mask needs. Never shorten a strong human mask into a
definition merely to hit an artificial word target. Never add a setup, punchline, abstraction,
or fake personality merely to make a REAL seem clever.

## Locked Examples

Good:

- `YOUR PHONE ALWAYS NEEDS IT`
- `EXTRAS ADDED TO THE BILL`
- `WHEN A BULL SEES RED`
- `LEFT WITH THREE KIDS AND RULES`
- `A GOOD LAWYER WILL BEAT IT`
- `RIGHT TO BEAR THEM`
- `ONE MORE NOTCH AFTER DINNER`
- `CINDERELLA’S MIDNIGHT DANCE`
- `OUTSIDE THE STRIKE ZONE`

Bad:

- `PHONE BEGS FOR OUTLET`
- `TOTAL JUMPS AT THE BOTTOM`
- `THE CAPE WAS A BAD IDEA`
- `HAIRCUT BETRAYS THE DECADE`
- `MENU WAITS BETWEEN TWO PEOPLE`
- `CHARGER LOST BEHIND THE COUCH`

Why the bad examples fail:

- `PHONE BEGS FOR OUTLET` uses fake object-acting instead of natural human phrasing.
- `TOTAL JUMPS AT THE BOTTOM` is abstract, writerly, and harder to picture than the meaning.
- `THE CAPE WAS A BAD IDEA` is commentary, not a playable mask.
- `HAIRCUT BETRAYS THE DECADE` is too writerly; people do not naturally phrase the idea that way.
- `MENU WAITS BETWEEN TWO PEOPLE` forces object-acting and sounds unnatural.
- `CHARGER LOST BEHIND THE COUCH` leaks the hero word's obvious root.

## Content First, Placement Second

Write and approve masks without targeting an Arc phase.

- Do not make a mask shorter, easier, harder, plainer, or more obscure because of `gpsTag`,
  `recommendedPhase`, round number, or intended placement.
- Every approved mask must stand on its own as fair, masked, playable POLYWORDS content.
- First approve the word's meanings, REAL masks, and traps.
- Only afterward may the Arc Generator place the audited word using separate familiarity,
  complexity, Snap, trap-sharpness, and phase-fit metadata.
- Placement metadata describes the word bank. It must not dictate the wording of a tile.

Do not use word count as a proxy for quality or difficulty. A longer mask is not automatically
harder, and a short mask is not automatically cleaner.

Avoid over-acted stage direction such as `GRIPS A PODIUM, CLEARS A THROAT` when the cleaner
cultural handle is `PRESIDENTIAL SPEECH`.

## Trap Standard

A trap is a guilty-close, legally wrong non-meaning.

Every trap must:

- bait one approved meaning direction;
- be close enough to cause hesitation;
- be wrong enough that a fair player cannot argue it is true;
- use the same natural human voice as REAL masks;
- match REAL masks closely enough in length, tone, and specificity that wording gives no answer
  away;
- include an editorial explanation of why it is tempting;
- use a recognized trap style.

Allowed trap styles:

- `neighbor`
- `scene`
- `tool`
- `result`
- `phrase`
- `sound-confusion`
- `almost-synonym`
- `sequence`
- `category`
- `family`

If a trap is actually true, promote it to a meaning when it is strong or remove it. Never leave
a secretly real tile marked false.

## Giveaway Root Rule

No tile may contain the hero word, an obvious inflection, or an obvious giveaway root.

For `CHARGE`, illegal words include:

- `CHARGE`
- `CHARGED`
- `CHARGING`
- `CHARGER`

Illegal:

- `CHARGER LOST BEHIND THE COUCH`

Legal nearby traps:

- `CORD LOST BEHIND THE COUCH`
- `BATTERY DOWN TO ONE PERCENT`
- `OUTLET BEHIND THE NIGHTSTAND`
- `SCREEN DIES DURING THE CALL`

Useful review questions:

1. Is this a true meaning of the word?
2. Is this a common phrase using the word?
3. Is it merely a nearby object, action, tool, result, or family member?
4. Could a fair player reasonably defend it as correct?
5. Does it feel random or unnaturally written?

## Hidden Meaning Standard

Hidden meanings are a boss/reward layer, not a dumping ground.

- Normal words do not hide strong ordinary meanings.
- Strong playable meanings belong in the ordinary REAL layer.
- Boss-hidden meanings must be surprising, fair, source-supported, and recognizable after
  reveal.
- A boss-hidden meaning is excluded from ordinary visible meaning counts and the visible Boss
  tile budget.

## Length, Casing, and Voice

- Store player-facing tile text in uppercase.
- Tiles may use up to eight words.
- Eight words is the hard maximum.
- Do not force short three- or four-word tiles.
- Use the full eight-word space when it improves playability, human voice, trickiness, or
  semantic disguise.
- Never add filler merely to make a tile longer.
- A short mask still needs disguise; a long mask still needs natural human language.
- Creativity is required, but abstract, poetic, cute, or weird wording is not a substitute for
  a legally clean mask.
- Fake object-acting is allowed only when the phrase is already natural, idiomatic, cultural,
  or genuinely funny.
- Clean punctuation is allowed when it serves a familiar phrase.
- Avoid fake-poetic object acting and language nobody naturally uses.
- Avoid repeating key nouns, giveaway roots, rooms, or semantic scenery across one word's bank.
- REAL masks and traps must have similar length, specificity, and tone so wording never reveals
  truth before commitment.

## Snap Ladder

A strong word should move from recognition into reinterpretation:

1. familiar meaning;
2. nearby second meaning;
3. pivot meaning;
4. rare-but-fair discovery when appropriate.

Every approved word should contain at least one meaningful context switch. If it has only
obvious meanings and no pivot, it is a weak POLYWORDS candidate.

## Human Approval Checklist

Before approving a word, confirm:

1. Every REAL is legally true.
2. Every meaning family is distinct.
3. Every trap is legally wrong and guilty-close.
4. Every tile sounds human.
5. No tile leaks the headword, an obvious inflection, or a giveaway root.
6. No tile relies on fake object acting.
7. No wording pattern reveals REAL versus trap.
8. At least one meaning creates a Semantic Snap.
9. Sources were checked, especially for rare or hidden meanings.
10. A player would blame the rushed judgment, not the writing.

Fairness beats cleverness. Clean clever is welcome only after semantic legality is secure.
