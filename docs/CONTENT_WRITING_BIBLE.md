# POLYWORDS

# Master Content Writing Bible

## Hunt Data, REAL Masks, TRAPS, Boss Words, Memory Snaps, Exceptions, and Editorial Standards

**Document purpose:** This is the consolidated working standard for
creating, reviewing, auditing, and approving POLYWORDS content.

**Status (2026-11-13):** Pete's master content document, added to the repo as
`docs/CONTENT_WRITING_BIBLE.md`. Sections 5, 11, 13, 19 and the new Section 16A were
amended the same day to match Pete's standing rulings (several REALs may share a meaning;
sound-alikes only when genuinely clever; traps never repeat a REAL's own scene; Pete's
binding trap rules). Where this document and an older note disagree, this document and
Pete's later rulings win.

**Governing philosophy:** POLYWORDS is a recognition game, not a
vocabulary lesson. The player already knows the meanings. The game
reveals how many different lives a familiar word has.

**Core emotional sequence:** Hesitation → Recognition → Satisfaction.

The ideal reaction is:

> "Oh... right. I knew that."

The stronger emotional target is:

> "Oh shit, yeah."

The player should feel that their own memory has switched on. They
should never feel that the game has ambushed them with obscure trivia or
an arbitrary interpretation.

# PART I. THE CONTENT PHILOSOPHY

## 1. Recognition, Not Definition

POLYWORDS does not ask players to learn unfamiliar dictionary
definitions. It asks them to recognize familiar meanings they may not
have consciously connected to the same word.

A dictionary establishes whether a meaning is legitimate. It does not
tell us how to write a good tile.

The writer's job is to find where that meaning lives in ordinary human
experience and bring the player close enough to recognize it.

Do not write a dictionary definition with a few words removed. Do not
write a synonym and call it a mask. Do not write an obscure clue merely
because it is technically accurate.

Write a memory trigger.

A successful tile produces a brief moment of uncertainty, followed by an
unmistakable connection. The player should think, "Of course. That's
what this is."

## 2. Every Meaning Lives Somewhere

Before writing a meaning, ask where it exists in a person's life.

Meanings may live in:

-   Everyday routines.
-   Work and money.
-   Sports and competition.
-   School and childhood.
-   Family and relationships.
-   Food, shopping, and household objects.
-   Technology and the internet.
-   Entertainment and popular culture.
-   Travel and public places.
-   Physical actions and sensations.
-   Familiar sayings and expressions.
-   Social behavior and shared experiences.

Think in memories, not dictionary categories.

A person does not usually remember a definition. They remember an ATM, a
paycheck, a river, a campfire, a sports game, a school assignment, a
familiar argument, or a moment when something happened.

Find the human experience that makes the meaning recognizable.

## 3. Every Word Has Its Own Voice

**The rules are consistent. The writing approach is word-specific.**

There is no universal writing formula for POLYWORDS. Every headword has
its own meanings, associations, cultural connections, natural contexts,
and strongest recognition opportunities.

Every word, every REAL mask, and every TRAP must be written specifically
for the headword being developed.

Do not force a word into the style used for the previous word. Do not
assume that a sentence structure, memory angle, trap style, or creative
technique that worked once will work for another headword.

Before drafting, understand the individual word:

-   Where do people encounter it?
-   What experiences naturally belong to it?
-   What memories does it activate?
-   Which meanings are familiar to ordinary speakers?
-   Which recognition angles are strongest for those meanings?
-   What kinds of false associations make this particular word tempting?
-   What writing style makes the content natural and playable?

One word may work best through a physical action. Another may need a
familiar expression, a social situation, a cultural reference, an
object, a sound, or a specific everyday scene.

Some words call for humor. Others work better when the writing is plain,
visual, conversational, nostalgic, occupational, or matter-of-fact.

The word determines the approach.

### The governing principle

Do not ask, "How did we write the last word?"

Ask:

**"What is the best, most natural way to write THIS word?"**

The standards govern quality and fairness. They do not impose a house
style on every headword.

## 4. Creative Judgment Is Essential

Following the rules does not automatically produce good content.

A tile can pass a mechanical checklist and still be dull, unnatural,
generic, or forgettable. Editorial judgment must evaluate the actual
human recognition experience.

Use the precision of a well-made crossword clue: approach the memory
indirectly, make each word earn its place, create brief productive
hesitation, and make the answer feel exact after recognition.

Do not import crossword obscurity, specialist trivia, abbreviations, or
complicated wordplay.

The goal is not to show how clever the writer is. The goal is to make
the player recognize something they already know.

# PART II. BUILDING THE HEADWORD

## 5. Start With a Meaning Audit

Do not begin by inventing clever wording.

First establish what the word actually means.

For each headword:

1.  Consult authoritative dictionaries.
2.  Collect the distinct senses relevant to the word.
3.  Compare definitions and merge duplicate or overlapping senses.
4.  Separate genuine meanings from phrases, collocations, extensions,
    associations, and examples.
5.  Identify which senses are familiar enough to be playable.
6.  Decide which senses belong in the intended game tier.
7.  Approve the playable meaning inventory before drafting REALs.

Dictionary validity is necessary, but it is not sufficient. A
technically valid sense may be too obscure, too regional, too
specialized, or too difficult to express naturally for the intended
audience.

Hold or exclude weak senses rather than forcing them into the game.

### Every REAL carries one distinct meaning; several REALs may share a meaning

Every REAL represents exactly one locked playable sense.

A sense may have more than one REAL when each one is a genuinely different,
strong recognition angle worth rotating (Pete, 2026-11-13: ADDRESS carries
three REALs for "where you live", two for "speech", two for "deal with it").
There is no quota: write each word to its natural depth, and add REALs and
traps when they are clever enough to earn a place.

Do not create extra REALs that only swap the example or reword the same
angle.

Do not inflate the sense inventory by treating every familiar phrase,
collocation, or use-case as a separate definition.

If two dictionary entries describe substantially the same playable
meaning, determine whether they should be merged. If they are genuinely
distinct meanings, preserve the distinction.

The test is semantic, not cosmetic: changing the example does not create
a new meaning.

## 6. Classify Playability and Difficulty

Use these broad fairness tiers.

### CORE

Most American English speakers should recognize the meaning after the
reveal. It belongs in ordinary play without specialist knowledge.

### ADVANCED

Many players will recognize it, but it is not universal. It may require
more experience, familiarity, or context.

### EXPERT / BONUS

Specialized, regional, archaic, technical, historical, or culturally
narrow material. Players should knowingly opt into this level of
difficulty.

Do not confuse dictionary legitimacy with core-game suitability.

## 7. Find the Human Handles Before Writing

Once the meanings are approved, identify the human handles or memory
surfaces for each one.

A handle is the real-life context that gives a meaning a place in the
player's memory. It may be:

-   A scene.
-   An action.
-   An object.
-   A sound.
-   A saying.
-   A social interaction.
-   A ritual or routine.
-   A physical sensation.
-   A recognizable event.
-   A cultural reference.
-   A familiar role or situation.

Handles are a discovery tool, not finished tile wording. They help
identify the strongest way into a meaning.

Do not settle for the first handle that comes to mind. Look for the most
natural, recognizable, specific, and useful angle for that headword.

# PART III. REAL MASKS

## 8. What a REAL Is

A REAL is a disguised expression of one legitimate meaning of the
headword.

It must point to that meaning through a human recognition trigger rather
than state the dictionary definition.

A REAL must:

-   Represent one legitimate, distinct meaning.
-   Use a natural human memory snap.
-   Be written specifically for its headword.
-   Disguise the meaning without removing the recognition trigger.
-   Sound like something a person would naturally say.
-   Create a brief, productive hesitation.
-   Become clear and satisfying on reveal.
-   Be fair for its assigned tier.
-   Avoid the headword and revealing derivatives.
-   Contribute a worthwhile recognition experience to the complete card.

A REAL should not require a speech from the writer to explain why it
counts.

## 9. How to Write a REAL

Start with the approved meaning. Identify where that meaning lives in
real life. Then choose the strongest writing approach for that
particular word and sense.

Possible approaches include:

-   A familiar situation.
-   A physical action or experience.
-   An everyday object.
-   A recognizable sound.
-   A common saying.
-   A social interaction.
-   A work or school context.
-   A sporting moment.
-   A cultural reference.
-   A routine or ritual.
-   A moment of emotion or consequence.

These are possibilities, not a template. Choose the angle that works
best for the actual headword.

A good REAL gives the player enough information to recognize the
intended meaning, but does not simply announce it.

### The reveal test

After seeing the answer, should an ordinary player be able to say:

-   "Right, of course."
-   "I know that use."
-   "I should have caught that."

If the likely reaction is "How was I supposed to know that?", the tile
is not ready.

## 10. Length Is Determined by the Memory Snap

There is no maximum word count for REAL masks or TRAP cards. Eight words
is not a cap.

Use as many words as the recognition moment genuinely needs to:

-   Sound natural.
-   Remain fair.
-   Identify the intended memory precisely.
-   Avoid accidental ambiguity.
-   Produce a strong recognition payoff.

Shorter is better only when it is equally effective.

Never weaken, flatten, distort, or make a tile unnatural to meet an
arbitrary word count.

Do not add words merely to make a tile sound clever. Every word should
contribute to naturalness, specificity, fairness, or recognition.

## 11. REAL Rejection Conditions

Reject or rewrite a REAL when:

-   Its supposed meaning is not a genuine distinct sense.
-   It repeats another REAL's meaning from the same angle (a cosmetic
    rewording rather than a genuinely different recognition experience).
-   It is technically valid but culturally invisible to the intended
    audience.
-   It requires specialist knowledge in ordinary play.
-   It sounds like a dictionary definition.
-   It is merely a synonym.
-   It has lost the human recognition trigger.
-   It could point to many unrelated concepts.
-   It requires a long explanation after the reveal.
-   It turns a phrase or collocation into a fake separate meaning.
-   It uses the headword or a revealing derivative.
-   It is unnatural, forced, or written to satisfy a formula rather than
    the word.

# PART IV. TRAPS

## 12. What a TRAP Is

A TRAP is a familiar association that tempts the player to connect it to
the headword, even though it is not one of that headword's meanings.

A good trap activates the wrong memory first.

The player should be able to understand why it felt plausible, then
recognize that it belongs somewhere else.

A trap is not random nonsense. It is not simply an unrelated word. It is
not another legitimate meaning disguised as a false answer.

The ideal reaction is:

> "I can see why I picked that."

## 13. Every Trap Must Be Specific to Its Headword

Build traps for the exact headword.

A trap may come from:

-   A neighboring concept.
-   A common association.
-   A familiar phrase that is not a true meaning.
-   Sound or spelling, only when genuinely clever. Avoid by default; it is
    "avoid", not "never" (Pete, 2026-11-12).
-   Imagery.
-   Cultural memory.
-   False category membership.
-   Counterpart words (a FALL card on SPRING: same family, different word).
    Never build a trap by flipping a REAL's own scene (Pete rejected
    "direct route / nonstop" against a scenic-route REAL).
-   An almost-meaning.
-   A familiar situation that people mentally connect with the word.

These are possible sources, not required categories. The headword
determines which trap approaches are natural.

A trap that works brilliantly for one word may be weak or nonsensical
for another.

Reject generic traps that could be pasted under many unrelated words.

## 14. Build an Independent Trap Pool

Develop one independent pool of traps for the headword as a whole.

Do not mechanically create one trap for every REAL.

Do not dress every trap in the scene of a corresponding REAL.

Do not manufacture a collection of mirrors, opposites, negations, or
predictable variations.

Opposition can be used when it independently creates a genuinely
tempting association, but opposites are not the default trap strategy.

The trap pool should contain the strongest false associations for that
headword, selected on their own merits.

## 15. The True-Owner Test

Every trap must have one clear true-owner word.

When the player sees the reveal or substitutes the true owner, it should
feel immediately correct.

For every proposed trap, record:

-   Why it tempts the target headword.
-   Why it is not a meaning of the target headword.
-   Which word genuinely owns the trap.
-   Why the trap becomes clear on reveal.

A valid trap must:

1.  Tempt the exact headword for a recognizable human reason.
2.  Be false of the target headword.
3.  Have one clear true owner.
4.  Feel natural and correct when assigned to that owner.
5.  Become clearly wrong for the target after examination or reveal.
6.  Avoid the target headword and revealing derivatives.

If the trap is actually another legitimate meaning of the target, it is
not a trap. It is a REAL, even if the writer intended it as a trap.

## 16. Trap Rejection Conditions

Reject or rewrite a trap when:

-   It is another legitimate meaning of the target.
-   It is obviously unrelated.
-   It depends on random association.
-   It relies on a cheap grammatical trick.
-   It is harder to understand than the REALs.
-   It is generic rather than word-specific.
-   It has no clear true owner.
-   The true owner does not feel right on reveal.
-   The player cannot understand why it was tempting.
-   It borrows a REAL's recognition trigger without a justified creative
    exception.
-   It uses the target headword or a revealing derivative.

## 16A. Pete's Binding Trap Rules

These are standing rulings. They apply to every ordinary and Boss trap.

1.  **Trap the word, not the situation.** The player must think the
    headword itself fits (DRIVE's coast / ride / ski / sail). Sitting in
    the same topic or scene is not enough.
2.  **One definite true owner** that is not the headword. Fair-player
    test: "Could a fair player protest and be right?" If yes, it is out.
3.  **Memory snap, not dictionary.** A trap that a dictionary could
    technically call the headword may stay if nobody actually talks that
    way.
4.  **The answer may not contain the headword** ("circuit breaker" for
    CIRCUIT).
5.  **No pull from a compound or set phrase containing the headword**
    ("direct deposit", "direct current" for DIRECT).
6.  **No pull through a related job title or derivative** (producer or
    choreographer cards baiting "director" for DIRECT).
7.  **Counterpart words are good; flipped REAL scenes are not.**
8.  **Never repeat a REAL's own scene or meaning** inside a trap. Generic
    shared words ("car", "saying") are fine. Any exception needs Pete's
    explicit approval for that card (see Section 19).
9.  **Bait the headword as a whole.** Never build a trap by answering or
    inverting one specific REAL.
10. **No sound-alikes or rhymes** unless genuinely clever.
11. **Search the whole bank first.** Before proposing a trap, check every
    word's cards for the same handle (ball four, gutter ball and alarm
    clock were already written on other words).
12. **Boss and gauntlet traps must pull hard.** A merely legal
    counterpart is not enough there.
13. **No new courtroom, judge or boxing scenes.** The bank already has
    many.
14. **Nothing that puts a group of people down, no profanity, no adult or
    STD content** (App Store age rating).
15. **Minimum three traps per word**, but never force a weak trap to fill
    a slot.
16. **Before rewriting a word, show every card it already has** (REALs
    and traps, with ids), marked keep / remove / new, so nothing
    duplicates an existing handle.

# PART V. THE HEADWORD RULE

## 17. The Headword Must Never Appear

The headword must never appear inside its own REAL or TRAP.

Also reject obvious revealing forms, including:

-   Inflections.
-   Tense variants.
-   Plural forms.
-   Adverb forms.
-   Compounds.
-   Derivatives.
-   Other forms that reveal the answer.

Judge semantically, not only by exact string matching.

The rule exists to protect the recognition experience. A tile must not
give away the answer through the headword itself or an obvious
variation.

**This rule is absolute. The general creative-exception rule cannot
waive it.**

# PART VI. ANGLE DIVERSITY, REUSE, AND CREATIVE EXCEPTIONS

## 18. Different Tiles Should Normally Create Different Recognition Experiences

Within one headword, each tile should normally approach its meaning or
association through a meaningfully different recognition angle.

Avoid unnecessary repetition of:

-   The same subject.
-   The same situation.
-   The same object.
-   The same scene.
-   The same sentence frame.
-   The same distinctive phrase.
-   The same key image.
-   The same cultural reference.
-   The same memory snap.

Changing a few words does not create a genuinely different tile.

The aim is not variety for its own sake. The aim is to give the player a
fresh recognition experience rather than repeatedly waking up the same
memory.

## 19. The Creative Exception: Clever Reuse Can Be Worth Keeping

The default is to avoid reusing the same handles, senses, situations,
objects, or memories when writing a word's REALs and TRAPs.

**But this is a default, not an inflexible prohibition.**

A repeated handle, situation, memory, or related recognition surface may
absolutely be used again when the new tile is sufficiently clever,
natural, and worthwhile.

Sometimes the strongest content comes from returning to a familiar
situation from a different angle. Sometimes a trap becomes especially
effective because it cleverly overlaps with a memory already used
elsewhere on the card. Sometimes the repeated context creates a better
recognition or misdirection experience than an entirely separate context
would.

Do not reject a strong tile merely because it shares a handle or memory
surface with another tile.

### Conditions for approving reuse

Reuse may be accepted when:

1.  The new tile contributes a genuinely worthwhile recognition or
    temptation experience.
2.  The overlap is deliberate, not accidental.
3.  The new tile is not merely a rewording of the earlier one.
4.  The repeated handle does not create a duplicate REAL meaning.
5.  The overlap does not make the card unfair or confusing.
6.  The result is stronger than the available alternatives.
7.  The reason for keeping the overlap can be clearly explained.

This exception can apply to:

-   REAL versus REAL handle reuse.
-   TRAP versus TRAP handle reuse.
-   REAL versus TRAP memory overlap, but only with Pete's explicit
    approval for that card. The default is Rule 8 in Section 16A: a trap
    never repeats a REAL's own scene or meaning.
-   Repeated situations, objects, subjects, imagery, phrases, or
    cultural references.

### What the exception does not permit

It does not permit:

-   Duplicate REAL meanings.
-   Lazy near-duplicates.
-   Repetition with no added value.
-   A trap that is actually another legitimate meaning.
-   A weak tile kept merely because it is difficult to replace.
-   Headword leakage or revealing derivatives.

### Record the reason

When reuse is approved, note what overlaps and why the new tile is worth
keeping.

The question is not simply, "Have we used this memory before?"

The question is:

**"Does using it again make this particular card better?"**

If yes, it may earn its place. If not, find a stronger alternative.

## 20. Exceptions Are Editorial Decisions, Not Loopholes

The rules protect quality, but they do not replace editorial judgment.

An exception may be approved when it produces a result that is clearly
stronger in one or more of these ways:

-   More human.
-   More natural for the headword.
-   Fairer.
-   More specific.
-   More memorable.
-   More playable.
-   More recognition-rich.
-   More clever without becoming obscure.

An exception must be deliberate and special, not merely convenient.

Record the rule involved, the reason for the exception, the alternatives
considered where useful, and why the exception improves the set.

The headword rule remains absolute. Duplicate REAL meanings remain
prohibited. Other writing and separation defaults may be relaxed when
the creative exception genuinely improves the content.

Final approval of exceptions belongs to the project owner.

# PART VII. BOSS WORDS

## 21. What Makes a Boss Word Different

Boss Words are not simply ordinary headwords with more tiles.

They are designed around additional hidden meanings and traps that
reward deeper recognition. The hidden content should feel like a genuine
discovery, not a forced attempt to make a word seem more complicated.

Boss content must meet the same meaning, fairness, naturalness,
headword, and trap-ownership standards as ordinary Hunt content.

Do not lower the standards to fill the Boss structure.

## 22. Boss Structure: Three Hidden Pairs

The current Boss Words structure is:

-   Three hidden REALs.
-   Three hidden TRAPs.
-   Three hidden REAL/TRAP pairs.

Each hidden REAL represents a legitimate, distinct meaning of the boss
headword.

Each hidden TRAP is a convincing false association that tempts the boss
headword but belongs clearly to another word.

The three pairs should create meaningful additional discoveries. They
should not be filler.

### Hidden REAL requirements

Each hidden REAL must:

-   Represent a genuine meaning of the headword.
-   Be distinct from the ordinary Hunt REAL meanings.
-   Be recognizable enough for its intended Boss difficulty.
-   Have its own natural memory snap.
-   Be written specifically for the headword.
-   Avoid the headword and revealing derivatives.
-   Produce a satisfying reveal.

### Hidden TRAP requirements

Each hidden TRAP must:

-   Be a false association for the boss headword.
-   Have one clear true owner.
-   Be tempting for a specific human reason.
-   Be distinct and worthwhile within the Boss set.
-   Avoid the headword and revealing derivatives.
-   Pass the same trap ownership and fairness tests as ordinary traps.

### Do not force three pairs

Three pairs are the intended structure, not permission to invent
meanings.

If the word does not have three additional legitimate, playable meanings
beyond its ordinary Hunt content, do not manufacture them.

If the additional meanings are weak, obscure, duplicative, or
unwritable, flag the problem. Recommend holding or demoting the word
from Boss status rather than padding the content.

A Boss Word must earn its status through the quality of its hidden
discoveries.

## 23. Boss Pairing and Reveal Quality

A hidden pair should feel coherent as a designed challenge, but the trap
must still be independently valid. Do not assume every trap must be a
direct opposite or mirror of its paired REAL.

For each pair, verify:

-   The hidden REAL is a distinct legitimate meaning.
-   The hidden TRAP is not another legitimate meaning of the target.
-   The trap has a clear true owner.
-   The pairing creates a fair and understandable challenge.
-   The two tiles do not reveal one another too easily.
-   The reveal adds a worthwhile discovery.
-   The pair contributes something the ordinary Hunt set does not
    already provide.

Pairing is a game-design relationship, not a license to weaken either
tile.

# PART VIII. WORKBOOK AND PRODUCTION RECORD

## 24. The Canonical Workbook

The current canonical content workbook is the production record for
approved content.

The workbook's `Tiles` sheet includes these fields:

-   `Word`
-   `Type`
-   `Tile`
-   `Meaning/Baits`
-   `Trap Style`
-   `Status`
-   `Notes`

Use the newest supplied canonical workbook when checking existing
content. Do not assume an older upload is still current.

Cross-reference proposed wording and distinctive memory snaps against
the workbook. Avoid recognizable recycling across different headwords
whenever a natural alternative exists.

Across different words, some real-life territory will naturally recur.
That is acceptable when the new tile is sufficiently distinct and does
not feel like recycled writing.

## 25. Status and Approval Discipline

Do not silently treat a draft as approved.

Use clear status language, such as:

-   Draft.
-   Under review.
-   Approved.
-   Locked.
-   Held.
-   Rejected.
-   Provisional.

If the current canonical workbook is unavailable, mark proposed content
**PROVISIONAL**.

Do not claim that a workbook comparison passed if the workbook was not
available and checked.

Do not update the workbook unless explicitly asked. When asked, use the
newest supplied workbook and only wording that has been approved.

Preserve locked wording. When revising one line, change only the line
under discussion unless a broader change is explicitly requested or a
serious conflict is identified.

# PART IX. THE REQUIRED WRITING WORKFLOW

## 26. Interactive Workflow for a New Headword

Follow this sequence when developing a new word with the project owner.

### Stage 1: Dictionary and sense audit

-   Research the headword.
-   Present the authoritative definitions.
-   Identify distinct senses.
-   Merge duplicates.
-   Separate meanings from phrases and associations.
-   Classify playability and difficulty.
-   Recommend which meanings should be included, held, or excluded.
-   Get agreement on the playable meaning inventory before drafting.

### Stage 2: Human handles

-   Identify where each approved meaning lives in real life.
-   Generate possible memory surfaces.
-   Explore multiple approaches when useful.
-   Select the strongest natural angle for each meaning.
-   Do not draft final masks prematurely.

### Stage 3: REAL drafting

-   Draft REAL masks only from approved meanings.
-   Write specifically for the headword.
-   Use the natural writing approach for that meaning.
-   Keep each line human, fair, and recognizable.
-   Review the complete REAL set together.
-   Check duplicate meanings and unnecessary angle repetition.
-   Preserve every line that has been locked.

### Stage 4: Independent trap development

-   Wait until the REAL set is stable.
-   Develop a separate trap pool for the entire headword.
-   Test each trap's temptation and true owner.
-   Reject traps that are actually REAL meanings.
-   Do not mechanically pair one trap with each REAL.
-   Consider creative reuse exceptions only when they improve the card.

### Stage 5: Boss development, when applicable

-   Confirm the ordinary Hunt meanings first.
-   Identify three additional hidden, legitimate meanings.
-   Draft three hidden REALs.
-   Build and test three hidden TRAPs.
-   Confirm that none of the hidden REALs duplicate ordinary meanings.
-   Do not force the structure if the word cannot support it.

### Stage 6: Full-card audit

Review the complete set together for:

-   Semantic accuracy.
-   Human recognition.
-   Fairness.
-   Natural language.
-   Angle and memory diversity.
-   Clever but justified reuse.
-   Headword leakage.
-   REAL/TRAP cross-contamination.
-   Trap ownership.
-   Difficulty balance.
-   Workbook repetition.
-   Overall game value.

### Stage 7: Approval and workbook update

-   Present the final audited content.
-   Identify any exceptions and explain them.
-   Obtain approval.
-   Update the workbook only when explicitly requested.
-   Preserve the approved wording and status.

## 27. One Surgical Revision at a Time

During interactive writing:

-   Do not casually rewrite approved or locked lines.
-   If one tile is under discussion, focus on that tile.
-   Do not reintroduce rejected wording without a good reason.
-   Do not change the meaning inventory while pretending to make a
    wording-only edit.
-   If a new discovery changes the underlying meaning audit, explain the
    change before revising the masks.
-   Keep the discussion linear and preserve decisions.

# PART X. COMPLETE EDITORIAL AUDIT

## 28. REAL Audit

For every REAL, ask:

1.  Is this a legitimate meaning of the headword?
2.  Is it distinct from every other REAL meaning on the card?
3.  Is it playable at the proposed tier?
4.  Does it activate a familiar human memory?
5.  Is the recognition angle the strongest natural one for this word?
6.  Does the wording sound human?
7.  Is it disguised without becoming obscure?
8.  Is it specific enough to point to the intended meaning?
9.  Does the reveal create recognition and satisfaction?
10. Is the length justified by the memory snap?
11. Does it avoid the headword and revealing derivatives?
12. Does it add a worthwhile experience to the complete set?
13. Is any repeated handle deliberate and worth keeping?
14. Has it been checked against the canonical workbook, or marked
    PROVISIONAL?

## 29. TRAP Audit

For every TRAP, ask:

1.  Is it genuinely not a meaning of the target headword?
2.  Does it tempt this exact headword?
3.  Is the temptation understandable to an ordinary player?
4.  Does it have one clear true owner?
5.  Does the true owner feel immediately correct on reveal?
6.  Is it natural and fair?
7.  Is it more than a random association?
8.  Is it independent of the REAL pool rather than a mechanical mirror?
9.  Does it avoid the headword and revealing derivatives?
10. Is any overlap with a REAL or another trap deliberate and
    worthwhile?
11. Does the trap create a satisfying "I can see why I picked that"
    moment?
12. Has it been checked against the canonical workbook, or marked
    PROVISIONAL?

## 30. Whole-Card Audit

Review the card as a complete experience.

-   Do the REALs represent distinct meanings?
-   Do the traps remain genuinely false of the headword?
-   Does every trap have a clear true owner?
-   Does the set feel written for this particular word?
-   Are the writing styles and angles appropriate to the word?
-   Does the card avoid formulaic repetition?
-   Are any repeated handles genuinely clever enough to justify their
    inclusion?
-   Are any overlaps confusing or unfair?
-   Is the difficulty appropriate?
-   Does the card create several distinct recognition experiences?
-   Does the set feel natural when read aloud?
-   Would an ordinary American English speaker understand the reveal?
-   Does the complete set deliver the POLYWORDS recognition experience?

## 31. Rejection Signals

Reject or reopen content when the likely player reaction is:

-   "How was I supposed to know that?"
-   "That wording doesn't sound human."
-   "That's just a dictionary definition."
-   "Those are basically the same meaning."
-   "That's actually another meaning of the word."
-   "That trap makes no sense."
-   "I can't see why that belongs to the other word."
-   "This feels like the writer is trying too hard."
-   "I've already seen this same memory again."
-   "The game is being clever at my expense."

A tile can be technically defensible and still fail as game content.

Never reduce review to a lint check. Mechanical checks support editorial
judgment; they do not replace it.

# PART XI. QUICK REFERENCE

## 32. The Rules in One Page

### REALs

-   Every REAL carries one approved meaning; several REALs may share a
    meaning when the angles are genuinely different. No quota.
-   Genuine meanings only.
-   Human memory snap, not dictionary wording.
-   Natural, fair, recognizable, and specific.
-   Written for the individual headword.
-   No arbitrary word limit.
-   Different recognition angles by default.
-   Reuse is allowed when genuinely clever and worthwhile.
-   Never include the headword or a revealing derivative.

### TRAPS

-   False of the target headword.
-   Tempting specifically for that headword.
-   One clear true owner.
-   True owner feels right on reveal.
-   Built as an independent word-level pool.
-   Not mechanically paired, mirrored, or opposed.
-   Human, natural, and fair.
-   Never repeats a REAL's own scene or meaning (exception only with
    Pete's approval for that card).
-   Follows Pete's binding trap rules (Section 16A).
-   Never include the headword or a revealing derivative.

### EXCEPTIONS

-   Rules protect quality; they are not a writing formula.
-   Word-specific execution is expected.
-   Clever reuse may be approved.
-   Exceptions must improve the actual player experience.
-   Duplicate REAL meanings are not allowed.
-   Headword leakage is never allowed.
-   Record why an exception earns its place.

### BOSS WORDS

-   Three hidden REALs.
-   Three hidden TRAPs.
-   Three hidden pairs.
-   Hidden REALs must be additional, distinct, legitimate meanings.
-   Hidden TRAPs must be false of the target and have clear true owners.
-   Never invent meanings to fill slots.
-   Demote or hold a word if it cannot support strong Boss content.

### WORKFLOW

1.  Audit meanings.
2.  Approve playable senses.
3.  Find human handles.
4.  Draft REALs.
5.  Stabilize the REAL set.
6.  Build an independent trap pool.
7.  Build Boss content if appropriate.
8.  Audit the complete card.
9.  Cross-reference the canonical workbook.
10. Obtain approval and update the workbook only when requested.

# PART XII. THE NORTH STAR

POLYWORDS is not teaching people words they do not know.

It is revealing the hidden network of meanings they have been using
their whole lives without noticing.

Every REAL should unlock the right memory.

Every TRAP should tempt the wrong memory for an honest reason.

Every headword should be written in the way that best expresses its own
natural meanings and associations.

Every exception should earn its place by making the content better.

The player should hesitate, recognize, and feel satisfied.

**Understand the word first. Find where it lives in people's minds. Then
write it in the way that gives POLYWORDS the strongest possible game
experience.**
